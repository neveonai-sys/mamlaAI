Section 1
Title: Injunction precedent overview
Indian injunction and specific-relief precedents repeatedly emphasize disciplined equitable reasoning. The recurring questions are whether the applicant has a serious claim worth preserving, whether interim protection is genuinely necessary, whether the applicant’s own conduct is equitable, and whether the proposed order is narrow enough to be workable.

Section 2
Title: Wander style appellate restraint note
This precedent pattern is often cited for the limited role of appellate interference in discretionary interim orders. For Mamla Brain, the practical takeaway is that an interim-order challenge should focus on perversity, misapplication of core tests, ignored material facts, or legal misdirection rather than simply re-arguing merits from the beginning.

Section 3
Title: Dalpat Kumar style injunction test note
The classic interim-relief formulation continues to matter because it separates a real arguable case from a final merits determination. The assistant should therefore avoid language that sounds like the case has already been conclusively won. Instead, it should explain whether the record presently supports a serious issue, comparative hardship, and a risk of non-compensable injury.

Section 4
Title: Best-seller and negative-covenant style note
In restraint and negative-covenant matters, courts are usually more willing to preserve the agreed negative obligation than to force a broad positive performance relationship at the interim stage. The practical use for retrieval is to spot confidentiality, exclusivity, and non-compete style questions where the remedy design may matter as much as the underlying breach.

Section 5
Title: Readiness and willingness in specific performance note
Specific-performance precedents consistently return to the claimant’s conduct. The case theory weakens if the claimant cannot show preparedness to perform, timely demand, financial capacity where relevant, and consistent pursuit of the contract. Mamla Brain should surface these conduct signals prominently when users seek coercive contractual enforcement.

Section 6
Title: Equitable conduct and delay synthesis note
Many injunction defeats arise not because the legal right is imaginary, but because the applicant delayed, tolerated breach, or asked for an order broader than the real necessity of the moment. Retrieval should therefore compare knowledge date, protest date, and filing date, and should help narrow the requested relief to the least excessive workable form.
